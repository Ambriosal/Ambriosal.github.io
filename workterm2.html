System Admin Assistant

UoG | Office of the Registrar • Winter 2026

For my second co-op position, I continued to work for my university but moved to the Office of Registrarial Services (ORS).

I worked in the Systems & Technologies department within the Registrar's Office of the University of Guelph. It was a small team of four (including me), that managed all systems (both hardware and software) of the Office. This office is a central point to all things related to the academic journey of students. It provides financial support, schedule courses, produces transcripts, enrollment services, and admission.

My major role was to provide software and hardware maintenance to the systems while also providing IT support to over 70 employees. I support the management of the systems and have security and network administration permissions to oversee software installation, group policy and network drives. I was also tasked to process daily student data in the client relations manager, Slate, to ensure email communication to prospective undergraduate students of the university.

When I first started my position, I created goals that I worked towards throughout my co-op position.

Technological Literacy

When starting this position, I knew I was going to be introduced to concepts and skills that I was unfamiliar with. So one of my goals was to expand my tech literacy in regards to networking, software & hardware administration and IT support.

My plan was to identify what specific areas of knowledge I needed to develop and focus on them. Then I would take the time to engage in hands-on learning and ask inquisitive questions to gain a personal understanding of the concepts.

Oral Communication

This job was different from my previous as this was a full-time in person role. A major part of my role was to be the front of systems IT issues for the office, which requires a great deal of oral communication to listen and understand the needs of the conversation. So my next aim to work towards was to improve my oral communications. My plan of action was to be an effective listener and approach in a friendly but also intentional manner to show to others that we are working together on the issue.

Problem-Solving

Since this role had IT support aspects, I knew having problem-solving skills was integral to my position and learning. I wanted to learn and expand my skill set in finding strategies to solve various problems. This would increase my independence and speed. I knew each problem would be unique but the general strategies to resolving situations would be similar, so learning how to optimise these strategies would be important.

Though my total co-op period was 8 months, I had two positions with different roles and responsibilities. This being my second co-op position allowed me to embrace these differences and have a deeper learning, not just technically but also personally.

Work

I realized quickly in this term that there were many skills and concepts that school didn’t teach me. This role wasn’t as technical (in terms of coding) as my previous position web developing but still needed a considerable amount of knowledge within the technology field. At first, it was overwhelming due to the start of the new semester and the nature of the registrar office. The school semester had started so lots of processes were in high-demand to commence and run smoothly.

There were a lot of different processes of my role to be aware of as it overarched all systems in the office. My daily tasks could vary a lot from a problem with a printer to raising permissions on selected systems. Though I believe I adapted to the spontaneity of the role, there was always a new problem that I was unfamiliar with.

One of my favourite takeaways from this role was that I was exposed to networking and systems administration. I’ve always had an interest in network administration and cybersecurity so this job furthered my affection for it. A responsibility of mine was to manage the organisational accounts of the office, so that means there were security protocols that I had to follow to ensure the integrity of the accounts. I felt a greater sense of responsibility for that, knowing how important security was in my role.

Office Culture

I truly loved the culture at the Registrarial Service! This office was much bigger in comparison to the Research services and my role was in-person so I developed lots more personal connections with varying departments all over the office. Also, I was one of two people for tech support so over the course of time, I met most of the faces within the office and created a friendly connection with them beyond simply just helping them with a problem. I also liked that the office had monthly cross-team meetings to get a sense of what the other departments were working on. These meetings were fun as we had special themes for Halloween and Christmas.

The office was fond of having office-wide events, which also in support of fundraisers! To list a few:

  • Soup-er Day: where you can bring any size bowl to have as much soup as you want! People within the office made their favourite soup to share.
  • Christmas Party Bake Sale: People brought in homemade treats and you could build a box of any!
  • ORS Wellness Cart: A cart filled with fun games and group activities. (My personal favourites were Sudoku, Uno and the Trivia)
Conclusion

Overall, this position was such a new experience that I developed real growth in my technical knowledge, my career path, and my personality. The hustle and bustle of the office kept me on my toes which allowed me to be involved and active within the office. I felt like I was a great contributor, not only to my team but to the office as well. I created great connections and long-lasting impressions in this community that I was a part of. The culture, the work and the food made this experience beautiful.

Reflecting back on my time there working for the Registrar office, I gained varying skills on different levels.

As mentioned before, my technological literacy increased specifically in networking and systems administration - a skill I hadn't learned in school. It was an in-person role with an emphasis on IT support so communication was vital for success. Lastly, problem-solving when met with unfamiliar situations and being able to adapt was also important. All these skills were strengthened and improved during this work term.

Technological Literacy

By the end of term, concepts that I had little to no knowledge in (networking, technical management, data-processing), was gained tremendously due to my active and hands-on learning. I believe my plan of consistently being engaged within my role helped deepen my understanding which I can use moving forward in both my personal life, and career.

Oral Communication

Being the first-responder for IT problems, my role and responsibilities relies heavily on effective oral communication. Asking the right questions, engaging in conversations beyond strictly work and communicating multiple solutions really helped developed my oral abilities and leadership.

Problem-Solving

Every day I was presented with problems that I had to solve creatively or think critically on how to solve unfamiliar when they were unfamiliar to me. Every day was a new problem, but as time went on, I was able to subconsciously develop general strategies that would lead me closer into finding solutions. This played a major part in my learning which helped me adapt quicker to each situation.
